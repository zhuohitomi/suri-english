Day 19Lost & Found

Lost & Found

Complete lesson

🎧 Listening · I Think I Left My Bag Behind

Practice passage

Hi, I was at your café about an hour ago, and I think I may have left a small shopping bag behind. Okay. Do you remember where you were sitting? Yes, I was at a table by the window, near the entrance. Let me check our lost and found. Can you describe the bag for me? Sure. It's a small white paper bag with black handles. There should be a jewelry box and a receipt inside. Give me one second. Actually, I think we have it. One of our staff found a bag by the window about twenty minutes ago. Oh, that's a relief. 📖 Reading · The Five-Minute Check I Do Before Leaving Anywhere

A few years ago, I developed a small habit whenever I travel: before leaving a café, restaurant, hotel room, or taxi, I stop for a few seconds and look around. The habit started after I left my phone in a restaurant during a trip. I had finished dinner, paid the bill, and was already several blocks away when I reached into my bag to check the map. My phone wasn't there. At first, I thought it might be somewhere at the bottom of my bag, so I went through everything I was carrying. After checking twice, I realized I'd probably left it on the table. I hurried back to the restaurant. Luckily, a server had noticed the phone almost immediately and put it behind the counter. Since then, I've created a simple routine. Before leaving a restaurant or café, I check the table, the chair, and the floor around me. Before checking out of a hotel, I check the bathroom, the bedside table, the closet, and every electrical outlet. And whenever I get out of a taxi, I take one last look at the seat. It sounds overly careful, but the whole routine takes less than a minute. It is especially useful when I'm traveling because I tend to carry more things than usual. When you're tired or in a rush, it's surprisingly easy to forget one of them. I've learned that remembering everything isn't really about having a great memory. Sometimes, it's simply about creating a habit that gives your brain one last chance to notice what you're leaving behind.
